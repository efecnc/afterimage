# Pour-Over Coffee Brewing

Pour-over coffee is a manual brewing method that emphasizes clarity, balance,
and reproducibility. It depends on four variables: grind size, water
temperature, brew ratio, and pour technique. Most recipes target a
coffee-to-water ratio of 1:16 by weight (for example, 20 grams of coffee to
320 grams of water).

## Equipment

A typical setup consists of a dripper (Hario V60, Chemex, or Kalita Wave), a
paper filter matched to the dripper, a gooseneck kettle for pour control, a
timer, and a scale that reads to 0.1 gram. Some brewers also use a separate
water kettle with temperature control, usually between 92 and 96 degrees
Celsius.

## Grind Size

Grind size is the first lever. For the V60, a medium-fine grind is standard:
slightly coarser than espresso, finer than drip. Too fine and the bed clogs,
producing a slow, bitter extraction. Too coarse and water rushes through,
producing a sour, weak cup. A good burr grinder is more important than the
dripper itself.

## Bloom

Every pour-over begins with a bloom. Pour about twice the weight of the dry
coffee in water over the grounds and wait 30 to 45 seconds. During the bloom,
carbon dioxide trapped in the freshly roasted coffee escapes, creating a
visible dome. Skipping or rushing the bloom traps gas in the bed, forcing
channels in subsequent pours and unevenly extracting the coffee.

## Pouring Technique

After the bloom, continue in two to four controlled pours. Aim for a steady
spiral from the center outward, avoiding the filter paper directly. The goal
is an even slurry: all grounds submerged for the same amount of time. A
common schedule is 60 grams at the bloom, 120 grams by 1:00, 240 by 1:45,
and the final 320 by 2:15, with total brew time landing between 3:00 and
3:30.

## Troubleshooting

If the cup tastes sour, grind finer, slow your pours, or raise water
temperature. If it tastes bitter, grind coarser, pour faster, or lower
temperature. Channelling shows up as uneven drawdown; keep the slurry flat
by pouring gently and at a consistent height. Log each brew so changes are
deliberate rather than superstitious.
